# Spirit Board

A talking board made of light, where an AI spirit answers. It opens in the
dark: dust drifts together into a single word, **START**, made of the same
glowing particles as everything else. Press it and START bursts, the particles
fly out past you, and they come down as the board's letters and numbers and a
candle, curved round you like the inside of a bowl. The planchette is your
pointer: wherever your mouse goes, its glass window goes. Rest on a letter for
a moment to spell it, and rest on the last one a little longer to send what you
spelled. Greet the spirit and the candle lights, a breeze gets up and Mavis
arrives: short-tempered, sassy, and not much of a talker. She takes the
planchette out of your hand, spells her answer on her own, usually in a single
word, and hands it back. Say goodbye and the board breaks up into a farewell:
a land of dust under the stars, with a stream of light along its ridge, where
a ghost made of particles holds the hand of a solid figure walking away, and
RESTART written underneath in the same particles as START. The idea comes from
Maxime Rivest's Tom Riddle diary for the reMarkable.

There is no typing anywhere. Even the word that unlocks the board is spelled
with the planchette. Nothing on the screen explains anything either: no hints
and no labels. Your message shows in white above the board, and the spirit's
replies glow there in candlelight. This README is the manual.

## How to play

- **Press START.** The dust gathers into START when the page opens, and waits
  there. Pressing it turns the sound on (browsers only allow sound after a
  touch) and the board flies together. Touching anywhere while it forms hurries
  it along.
- **The planchette is your pointer.** Move the mouse and the planchette's
  window follows it, centered on it; you never need to click or drag. On a
  phone, touch the board and slide: the window sits just above your fingertip
  so your finger doesn't hide the letter.
- **Spell by resting.** Hold the window still on a letter for a moment and the
  letter is added to your message at the top; a ring round the window fills as
  you rest. With a mouse you can also click a letter to take it at once.
  Letters run together the way they do on a real board (`WHATISYOURNAME`), and
  the spirit reads them fine. To spell a double letter, move off the letter and
  back on. Tap your message to rub out its last letter.
- **Send by resting a little longer.** Keep the window still on the last letter
  for two seconds, while a second, fainter ring fills, and your message goes.
- **Unlock the board.** A new device starts locked. Spell the secret word and
  rest on its last letter. A wrong word sends the planchette to **NO**. Each
  device stays unlocked for 30 days.
- **Greet the spirit.** The candle is out and only faint moonlight falls on the
  board. Spell a greeting and rest: `HI`, `HELLO`, `HEY`, `ISANYONETHERE`,
  `WHOSTHERE`, or a hello in another language (`HOLA`, `SALAM`, `BONJOUR`...).
  Anything that isn't a greeting fades into the dark board and nothing comes. A
  greeting lights the candle, a breeze gets up, and the spirit greets you back,
  differently each time, depending on her mood tonight (`WHAT`, `YOU AGAIN`,
  `FINALLY`, `WHO CALLS`...).
- **Ask anything.** The spirit answers in a word when she can, and in two or
  three only when one won't do. A yes-or-no question gets a bare **YES** or
  **NO**, always plain: never NOPE or YEAH. With nothing spelled, resting on
  **YES** or **NO** answers her own question straight away. Ask `WHOAREYOU`
  and she spells her name. Ask to see her (`SHOWYOURFACE`, `SHOWYOURSELF`) and
  she spells **GOODBYE** and leaves. While she thinks and spells, the
  planchette is hers: moving your mouse does nothing, and a faint dot shows
  where your pointer is. When she's done, the planchette slides back to your
  pointer.
- **End the séance.** Rest the window on **GOODBYE** and the spirit leaves
  without a word: a last gust blows the candle out, and the board breaks up
  into the farewell. The far dunes form first, then the stream of light along
  the ridge, then the ghost, and last the figure holding its hand, from the
  feet up. Then RESTART gathers under the land, written like START, and stirs
  as the planchette comes near. She also leaves on her own if you're rude, or
  if you ask to see her.
- **Restart.** Press RESTART and it bursts, and the land flies straight back
  into the board, candle out, ready for another greeting. It doesn't go back to
  START.

## The spirit

There is one spirit, Mavis, and she answers on Claude Opus 5.5. She has been
stuck answering this board for longer than she cares to say and is fed up with
silly questions: dry, impatient and sassy rather than spooky (`OBVIOUSLY`,
`UGH`, `RUDE`, `NEXT`), never cruel, and secretly glad of the company. A no is
always a plain **NO**. Each séance she's in one of eight moods (woken from a
doze, busy with something better, suspicious, in a rare good mood that annoys
her...), which sets how she greets you and her temper for the rest of it. She
moves the planchette briskly, straight at each letter and a little past it,
and the candle burns in plain candlelight.

## The board, the light and the wind

- **The board wraps round you.** Its sides come toward you and its middle sinks
  back, so it curves like the inside of a bowl instead of standing flat in
  front of you. The candle and the letters at the edges are nearer and bigger.
  The view sways a little toward your pointer (and by itself on a phone), so
  the curve and everything behind it slide past one another.
- **Light.** Faint, cold moonlight falls from high beyond the board's far top
  corner, so the letters are brighter toward **NO** and fade toward the
  candle's side. The candle's warm light falls off with distance, so only the
  letters near it glow. Each stroke catches more light on the edge facing a
  light, and no two grains catch it alike: most are dull, a few glint, and each
  letter is a little different. The flame flickers, gutters now and then, bends
  and dims in gusts, and the planchette throws a soft shadow away from it.
- **Wind.** Still until the spirit comes; then a breeze that wanders, gusts
  that roll across the board from the candle's side, and eddies carried along
  with them. Letters lean as each gust passes and swing back after. The
  loosest grains lift off the strokes and stream away downwind, more of them
  the harder it blows, and come back to go again, so the letters stay
  readable. Dust in the air drifts with the wind and glints near the flame,
  and heat and smoke rise off the flame and bend away downwind.
- **When the spirit speaks,** the flame flares up taller and whiter, its light
  reaches further, and the wind rises with it. You can hear the wind too.
- **Space.** Behind the board, streams of smoke flow along the top and the
  bottom of the view and far back across the middle, winding and twisting as
  they go. Dust flows past at three depths, and a few motes drift so close to
  your eye that they're out of focus.

## Run it on your computer

You need [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) 22.12 or newer.

```bash
npm install
cp .dev.vars.example .dev.vars   # then edit it
npm run dev                      # open the URL it prints
```

In `.dev.vars`:

- `BOARD_WORD` is the word that unlocks the board. Use letters or digits;
  5–8 letters works well.
- `COOKIE_SECRET` can be any long random string.
- `MOCK_SPIRIT=1` plays with canned replies and needs no API key. This is good
  for trying it out.
- For the real spirit, set `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` to a key from the
  [Anthropic Console](https://console.anthropic.com) and set `MOCK_SPIRIT=0`.

To play on your phone over Wi-Fi, run `npm run dev -- --host` and open the
network URL it prints.

## Put it online for your friends (free)

### On Vercel

The repository is ready for Vercel: `vercel.json` tells it to run
`npm run build:vercel`, which builds the page and the API.

1. In Vercel, add a project from this GitHub repository (or open the one you
   already linked).
2. In the project's **Settings → Environment Variables**, add:
   - `BOARD_WORD`: the word friends spell to unlock the board (letters or
     digits, 5–8 letters works well)
   - `COOKIE_SECRET`: any long random string
   - `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`: your key from the
     [Anthropic Console](https://console.anthropic.com)
3. Redeploy. Vercel builds every push; the branch it treats as Production is
   set under **Settings → Git**.

Send your friends the project's `.vercel.app` link and the board word. Rate
limits on Vercel are counted per running instance, so they're looser than on
Cloudflare; the Anthropic spend limit below is the real cap.

### On Cloudflare

The app also runs on a Cloudflare Worker, which is free at this size.

1. Create a free account at [cloudflare.com](https://dash.cloudflare.com/sign-up).
2. Log in from this folder:

   ```bash
   npx wrangler login
   ```

3. Store the three secrets. Each command asks you to paste a value:

   ```bash
   npx wrangler secret put BOARD_WORD
   npx wrangler secret put COOKIE_SECRET
   npx wrangler secret put ANTHROPIC_API_KEY
   ```

4. Deploy:

   ```bash
   npm run deploy
   ```

The command prints a link like `https://spirit-board.<you>.workers.dev`. Send
your friends the link and the board word. To change the word later, run
`npx wrangler secret put BOARD_WORD` again.

## What it costs

The spirit answers on Claude Opus 5.5 at low effort. Replies are a word or two,
and her instructions are cached between questions, so most of the cost is the
model's brief thinking before it answers: about $6 per 1,000 questions, well
under a cent a question. A whole séance of a dozen questions costs a few cents.

Safety nets:

- **Spend limit (do this first).** In the Anthropic Console, set a monthly
  spend limit under *Settings → Limits*. That's the hard cap.
- **Rate limits.** Each device can ask about 15 questions a minute. Wrong
  board words are limited to 5 tries a minute per network. On Cloudflare you
  can change both under `ratelimits` in `wrangler.jsonc`.
- **Another model.** Set `SPIRIT_MODEL` (a var in `wrangler.jsonc`, or a Vercel
  environment variable) to answer on another model, for example
  `claude-haiku-5-5` to cut the cost to about $0.20 per 1,000 questions. Leave
  it unset for Opus.

Two more things to know:

- **Refusals.** The spirit asks with server-side fallbacks turned on: if
  Claude's safety checks decline a question, another model answers it in the
  same call. On Haiku, which has no fallback, a declined question makes her
  spell `I CANNOT SAY`.
- **Slow answers.** If Opus hasn't answered within 12 seconds, or Claude is
  overloaded, Haiku answers instead, so a séance never stalls. In the
  claude.ai preview the wait is 9 seconds.

## Tune the spirit

The spirit has two halves:

- `shared/presence.ts` holds what everyone sees: her name, the candle's color,
  her motion style and her sound. The motion style sets how she moves the
  planchette: its speed, how much its paths bow, how unsteady it is, how long
  it rests on each letter, how far it slides past a letter, and how long it
  pauses before setting off.
- `worker/spirits.ts` holds what only the server knows: the model she answers
  on, her voice, a secret, how she brushes off questions too big for a board,
  and her example exchanges, the first of them a greeting. The examples set
  the tone more than anything else.

The rules she follows are in `BASE_RULES` in the same file: answer the
greeting in a way that gets the sitters asking, one word (two or three only
when one can't carry it), a plain YES or NO alone for a yes-or-no question,
leave when insulted or asked to show herself, sassy but never cruel, and gentle
guardrails. Her eight moods are `moods` in the persona; the page picks one when
the séance begins and sends it with every question. What counts as a greeting
is `isGreeting` in `shared/text.ts`. Two rules are also kept in code, whatever
the model says: `sanitizeReply` turns NOPE, NAH, YEP and YEAH into a plain NO
or YES, and asking to see her (`asksToSeeSpirit`) gets GOODBYE without asking
the model at all.

To hear how she actually sounds before you film:

```bash
npm run sample                                  # on Opus (about 30 cents)
npm run sample -- --effort medium               # with more thinking
npm run sample -- --model claude-haiku-5-5      # on another model, to compare
```

This greets her three ways in each of her moods, then asks the same 21
questions after a `HELLO`, through the real model, and prints the replies with
what the run cost. It flags any reply that breaks the board's format. It reads
`ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` from your shell or from `.dev.vars`. The unit tests check
that her lines fit on the board.

## How it's built

- **Front end** (`src/`) is plain TypeScript built with Vite, with
  [Three.js](https://threejs.org) for the particles. It has no framework.
  - The board layouts are data (`src/board/layout.ts`): two arcs of letters
    on wide screens, three arcs on phones held upright.
  - Every glyph and the candle are drawn into a mask and sampled into points
    (`src/scene/masks.ts`, `src/scene/sampling.ts`). The points crowd the
    edges and run in short threads through the strokes, which keeps the
    letters legible.
  - The same points make every picture (`src/scene/board.ts`): loose dust,
    START with dust swirling round it in a tilted disc, the board, and the
    farewell. Each point knows its place in each picture, and the shader flies
    it from one to the next along a curve that swings out toward the eye, so
    nothing is moved on the CPU.
  - The farewell is laid out in a land of its own (`src/scene/land.ts`), seen
    by an eye standing a little way off. The dunes, the hill and the ridge
    were traced from where they should show on screen and projected back onto
    the ground, so the land lands where it was drawn on any screen. The two
    figures (`src/scene/figure.ts`) are a few ellipses, tapered limbs and
    smooth outlines, filled with points. The ground's dust is spread evenly
    where the eye can see it, so it crowds along the crests, and an extra
    layer of dust (`src/scene/dust.ts`) covers what the board's points can't.
    It is all worked out a few milliseconds at a time while START waits, and
    framed smaller and moved to the middle on a phone held upright. RESTART,
    under the land, is written in grains of its own, from the same lettering
    and as thickly as START.
  - The board's curve (`src/scene/bend.ts`) is one small formula, shared by the
    shader that bends the particles and the page, which runs it backward
    (with a few steps of Newton's method) to find the point of the board under
    the pointer, through the camera as it sways.
  - The wind (`src/scene/wind.ts`) is a mean breeze, gusts that travel
    across the board, and curl noise for eddies, so the air swirls without
    piling up. The dust and the motes off the flame are moved through it on
    the CPU (`src/scene/air.ts`) and drawn as short streaks. The shaders
    (`src/scene/shaders.ts`) read the same gusts, so each letter leans as a
    gust passes and springs back after.
  - The candle's light (`src/scene/light.ts`) flickers on noise rather than
    on a loop, gutters, bends with the wind and flares while the spirit speaks.
    The shaders light every point by the moon and the candle, each with an
    inverse-square fall-off and more where its stroke's edge faces the light.
    The flame, the glow and the planchette are soft gradients, not particles.
  - Behind it all, stars drift and the sky turns very slowly
    (`src/scene/sky.ts`), dust flows through space at three depths
    (`src/scene/space.ts`), and streams of smoke wind past
    (`src/scene/streams.ts`): ribbons of specks that the shader twists, folds
    and frays as they flow.
  - The camera sways toward the pointer with its lens shifted to match, so the
    board's plane never moves while nearer and farther layers slide past it
    (`src/scene/stage.ts`). On a device that can't keep up, the scene drops its
    resolution a step at a time instead of stuttering.
  - The planchette follows the pointer (`src/planchette/planchette.ts`), and
    the spirit moves it the way fingers do (`src/planchette/possess.ts`): a
    pause before it sets off, a smooth glide that bows a little, a slide past
    the letter and a pull back.
  - Every sound is synthesized with Web Audio, so there are no audio files.
- **Server** (`worker/api.ts`) answers three endpoints: `/api/me`,
  `/api/enter` and `/api/ask`. It checks the signed cookie, validates and
  cleans everything it receives, and calls Claude through `@anthropic-ai/sdk`.
  The same code runs as a Cloudflare Worker (`worker/index.ts`) or as Vercel
  Functions (`vercel/handler.ts`, built by `scripts/build-vercel.ts`).
- **Preview** (`npm run build:artifact`) builds a one-file copy for a
  claude.ai artifact, where the spirit answers through the viewer's own
  Claude.
- **Shared** (`shared/`) holds the rules for what the board can spell and the
  spirit's public data.
